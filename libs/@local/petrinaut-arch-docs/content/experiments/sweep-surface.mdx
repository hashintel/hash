The surface view shows one metric's final value over two swept parameters as
a filled contour, Optuna-style. It is fed by the same combination cache the
[navigator](doc:experiments/parameter-sweeps) refines: a point on the surface
is a point the sweep computed, and nothing else.

![How the surface fills](@diagrams/sweep-surface-feed.svg)

## The sweep feeds the surface

The surface samples nothing. The sweep session folds every finished batch of
a **point** selection into its cache and publishes the point as a visited
cell — its quantized position and each metric's value over the finished runs
(`SweepVisitedCell`, on `record.sweep.visited`, a new array only when a batch
folds). Points arrive three ways: the navigator's controls collapsing to a
point, a click or drag on the plot, and an optimizer navigating trial by
trial through `navigateSweep`. A range selection folds into the cache too but
is no point, so it draws nothing.

The component (`sweep-surface.tsx`) turns the visited list into a field with
the pure helpers beside it (`sweep-surface/visited-field.ts`):
`visitedSurfaceField` projects every visited point onto the two shown axes at
its fractional grid coordinate (`surfaceGridCoordinate`, from
`shared/surface-field.ts`), a filled dot with the
shown metric's value, the point matching the selection emphasized, a muted
ring where the metric never reported. `computingSurfaceField` adds the point
being computed as a ring and nothing more: its value joins the field from
the visited list once the batch folds, so the contour never chases the
metric's time course while a batch streams. Points computed at other values
of the hidden axes are drawn as well, projected, the way a study's trials
are: the picture is everything computed so far, not a slice. Switching the
shown metric re-reads the same visited cells. The caption
(`describeVisitedSurface`) words what computes as the navigator does — the
selected point, or a sampling across the selected ranges — and names the
step's point while an optimizer drives.

The plot is a control in both directions: a dark ring marks the navigator's
position, and a press-drag shows a CSS crosshair (positioned in percentages,
so pointer moves never repaint the field raster) with a live value readout in
the caption; releasing collapses every axis to a point, exactly as a click
does: the two shown axes at the pick, each hidden axis at the middle of its
range, so a pick is always a point the surface can draw. While an optimizer
drives the sweep the card takes the `optimizing` tone and the plot gets no
pick handler, so it only displays.

## Rendering sparse data honestly

At any moment most cells are unsampled, so the picture must make sense at
every stage. Inverse distance weighting over the sampled points gives smooth
blobs around the first few samples that converge to the surface as cells
land; marching squares over that raster draws the iso-lines, and sampled
combinations are marked with dots so it stays visible where data exists. All of that is pure, tested math in
`ui/components/contour-surface/contour-field.ts`; the component only paints it onto a
canvas, the same imperative-draw idiom the metric charts use.

An axis or metric change clears the field. The plot
keeps the previous picture up, dimmed to 45%, whenever fewer than three fresh
samples exist (one or two samples interpolate to a near-uniform wash that says
less than the old picture does), with the new dots drawn on top; a blank plot
after every slider move would read as a crash. This is the same bridge the
metric charts build with a snapshot overlay, built here by retaining the last
complete-enough field. The `Components / ContourSurface → Restarting` story
demonstrates the cycle.

## A point's value is its ladder depth

A visited point's value is the mean over the last _sampled_ frame's
distribution of the runs that have folded — a terminating net finishes its
runs before `maxTime`, so the final frames legitimately carry no samples, and
reading only the literal last frame once discarded every point of such a
net's surface. A point the navigator stays on climbs the ladder to the run
budget; a point an optimizer visited stops at the trial's cap (eight runs)
until the study settles on it or the navigator returns to it, and the deeper
result flows back into the same cache and the surface repaints with it.

## The optimizer on the surface

A study started with the experiment from the Create Experiment drawer draws
on this same surface and no other: its steps are points the sweep visited through `navigateSweep`,
so each lands as a dot at the trial's cap of eight runs, the step in flight
is the computing ring, and infeasible draws never reach the surface because
the evaluator prunes them before it navigates. While the study drives the
sweep the card takes the `optimizing` tone and only displays; once the study
settles, or Stop on the Parameters card ends it, the navigator parks on the
best point, the plot takes picks again, and the point refines to the run
budget through the same cache.
[Sweep orchestration](doc:experiments/sweep-orchestration) describes the
study that drives that navigation and the constraints it enforces.
