A net composes components, components drawn once and instantiated many times,
by fusing places: a place of the net and ports of its instances become one
place when the net runs. This page is the proposal behind the code in
`petrinaut-core/src/composition/`, written as that code stands. The user
guide's Composition page says how the editor exposes it.

![Place fusion, drawn and flattened](./composition-fusion.d2)

## Why fusion, and why keep port arcs

The first version of components connects a component to its surroundings with
arcs only: a transition of the containing net reads from or writes to a port,
a place inside the instance, through a `componentPort` arc endpoint. That
works for a component used by one net, but it makes every connection pass
through a transition of the containing net, and two components can only
exchange tokens through such a transition. A buffer shared by two machines
needs a place of its own.

Place fusion is the classical answer: a fusion set names places that are one
place. Petrinaut's version keeps it small on purpose.

- A fusion belongs to the net that holds the instances. Its members are
  places of that net and ports of those instances, named the way an arc
  endpoint names them (`FusionMember = ArcEndpoint`), so the endpoint helpers
  and the id canonicalization apply unchanged.
- At most one member is a place of the net itself: the **anchor**. It gives
  the fused place its id, its initial marking and its visualizer, and it
  stays the place metrics and scenarios refer to. The other members are
  ports. A fusion without an anchor, two ports joined directly, makes a
  place that exists only inside the composition.
- A fusion has a name of its own, and goes by no other: the checks'
  messages, the assistant's approval card and the flattened net say it,
  whatever its anchor is called. `addFusion` names a
  fusion that comes without one `Fusion N`, with the lowest number no fusion
  of its net uses. A file whose fusion has no name does not load, and
  `updateFusion` renames a fusion but does not take its name away.
- Fusions are sets: a member belongs to one fusion, and a connection that
  joins two fusions merges them. `addFusion`, `addFusionMember` and
  `replaceFusionMember` absorb a fusion that already holds one of the
  members; a member removed from a fusion of two members removes the fusion.
  `replaceFusionMember` moves a fusion from one member to another as one
  change, which is what dragging the end of a fusion link does. A document
  from elsewhere can still list a member in two fusions, which the checks
  report and flattening refuses: the two would chain into one place.
- Removing a place, an instance, a component, or a port's `isPort` mark takes
  it out of every fusion that named it, the way arcs on it go away.

Port arcs stay. A transition's arc to `Instance.Port` means the same thing
as an arc to the place that port is fused with: flattening resolves the port
to its scoped place and the fusion then maps that place onto the fused one,
so the two mechanisms compose. The two differ only in what the canvas shows,
a place of the net that the user can mark, watch and measure, against a port
reachable through its instance alone. Nothing forbids using both on one port.

## Ports and their directions

A port is a place of a component marked `isPort`. A port may also declare a
direction, `in`, `out` or `inout`, which is a promise about how the
component uses it, seen from inside the component:

| Direction | The component's transitions… | Typical use                       |
| --------- | ---------------------------- | --------------------------------- |
| `in`      | only consume from the port   | what the component works on       |
| `out`     | only produce into the port   | what it hands on                  |
| `inout`   | both                         | a shared resource pool, a counter |

A port without a declaration takes the direction its arcs give it, `inout`
when none moves a token through it. Only arcs that move tokens count, here
and in the checks: a read or an inhibitor arc looks at the port and takes
nothing, so it makes no port an input. The arcs are those of the component's
own transitions, on the port and on whatever the component fuses it with
inside, which is the same place: a port fused with a port of an inner
instance that a transition of the component produces into is an output,
though no arc reaches the port itself. What the inner instance does with its
own port is not counted. The checks hold a declaration against the same arcs
and report a port written to that declares `in`. The direction has no
effect on what a fusion may join: a port is a place, and a place has no
direction. It decides three things.

- **Where the port draws**: an input port's dot and label sit on the left of
  the instance, an output port's on the right, a two-way port's on both.
- **Dataflow checks**: a member port counts as a producer unless its
  direction is `in`, and as a consumer unless it is `out`. The warning and
  the note described under "What members must agree on" are worked out from
  that.
- **Interfaces**: a component's ports with their directions and token types
  are its signature, which an
  [interface](doc:core.composition/interfaces-and-fusions) holds its
  implementations to.

What two ports can and cannot be joined:

- `out` to `in` is the pipe. `out` to `out` is a merge; `in` to `in` is a
  shared source that something else has to fill. Both are allowed, and the
  dataflow checks say what is missing.
- Two ports of the same instance may be fused; it closes a loop through the
  component. Allowed, and it flattens unless a transition inside the
  component touches both ports with arcs of one kind.
- A port can be fused only with places and ports of the net that holds its
  instance. To reach a port of a nested instance from further out, the
  containing component declares a port of its own and fuses it with the
  nested port inside; the flattener follows the chain. The test in
  `flatten-component-instances.test.ts` named "applies fusions declared
  inside a component to the nested instances" shows the forwarding.

## What members must agree on

The fused place is one place, so its members must describe one place.
`checkFusions` reports, as errors, members that no longer resolve, a
duplicate member, a member another fusion of the net holds too, a second
anchor, a fusion of fewer than two members, a transition that reaches the
fused place twice, and the three disagreements:

- **Token type**: compared structurally, the same elements in the same
  order under the same names and value types, because a component's type and
  the net's type are different `Color` entities even when they say the same
  thing. The packed token layout is what has to match.
- **Capacity**: two declared capacities that differ. A declared capacity
  beside unbounded members bounds the fused place.
- **Dynamics**: two members whose dynamics compose different equations. One
  member's dynamics drive the fused place; two instances of one component share
  their equations and agree. An equation is the one its own net defines, so
  two nets that each define an equation under one id disagree. The fused
  place runs them once, with the parameter values of the first member that
  has them.

A transition's code addresses its arcs by position, so two arcs of one kind
from one transition onto members of one fusion cannot be folded into a
single arc: that is the repeated-arc error, and the fix is to keep one of
them.

Two more conditions are reported below error severity: a fusion in which
every member only consumes gets a warning, nothing puts tokens into it; one
in which every member only produces gets a note, it accumulates. Arcs of the
containing net on the anchor, and port arcs on the member ports, count as
producers and consumers too.

Flattening throws on each of these errors too, apart from a duplicate member
and a fusion of fewer than two members, which merge nothing, so a document
the editor flags as broken does not simulate with a surprise.

The checks read one fusion of one net at a time. Flattening merges across
nested instances, so it can also refuse what no single fusion shows: a
capacity or dynamics that disagree along a chain of fusions through a
nested instance, and a transition inside a component that reaches two of its
own ports once the net outside fuses them. The run then stops with the
flattener's message.

## Flattening

`flattenComponentInstancesForSimulation` scopes every entity of an instance
under its id, `Instance::entity`. It collects each fusion, at each net it
walks, as a group of flat place ids, then `applyFusions` runs union-find
over the groups. The place with the fewest `::` separators leads its set, so
a root anchor keeps its id, and the scoped ones merge into it.
Merging moves every arc onto the leader, takes a member's capacity onto the
leader, gives a leader with no dynamics the equations of the first member
that runs some, along with the parameter values they read, and drops the
merged places.

A fusion without an anchor keeps the first port's scoped place as the
leader. Either way the leader is renamed after its fusion, so the timeline
shows the fused place under the name the canvas gives the fusion. Where a
port is fused inside its component and again in the net above, the leader
takes the name of the outer fusion.

A component of an import is flattened as a component of the document is: the
net the flattener is given has each one inlined by `inlineImports`, under ids
that are the same on every call, so its body is scoped under the instance's
id like any other.

## Where the model stops today

- **Component libraries** are imported by reference, with no version: a
  document names a library and its components by id, and the host resolves
  them (see [Host integration](doc:core.handle/host-integration)). A body of
  a library's component holds places and transitions, not instances of
  other components. A root net marked as composed from components alone is
  not there yet.
- **Initial markings** address root places. A component with internal
  state, a pool of workers say, has no marking of its own. A per-instance
  marking, or markings written into the component, is the next step.
- **Metrics** read root places. A port-only fusion is measurable only through
  the timeline's flattened places. Letting metrics name `Instance.Port`
  would follow the naming the arcs already use.
- **The GPU path** does not flatten instances yet, so a composed net runs on
  the CPU engine.
- **Clipboard**: copying instances copies no fusion. Fusions among copied
  instances could come along.
- **Interfaces** are in, with the implementation chosen per instance or by
  the document's default; a choice per scenario and implementations a host
  provides are the next layers, in
  [Interfaces, injection and the three fusions](doc:core.composition/interfaces-and-fusions).
- **Layout**: fusion links are edges for auto-layout, so fused nodes land
  near each other, but a port's side is not yet a layout constraint.
