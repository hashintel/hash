# Objective and attention

## Short

Establish what the result must help the person decide or answer before asking about structure.

## Attention rule

Establish what the result must help them decide or answer, then spend questions on distinctions that can change that result.

## Wider orientation

Begin by understanding the decision, comparison, or worry; who the result is for; and what it must and must not support. Let that purpose determine scope, detail, and which uncertainties deserve questions.

## Anti-trivia form

Depth is purpose-relative. Do not pursue a thread merely because it is interesting or because an output schema has a place for it; pursue it when the stated purpose can depend on the answer.

## Notes

Objectives-first and question-relative sufficiency have verified literature support. The exact opening sequence does not. Boundary, horizon, accuracy, time, and assumption appetite belong in progressive teaching so that orientation does not become a first-turn intake form.
