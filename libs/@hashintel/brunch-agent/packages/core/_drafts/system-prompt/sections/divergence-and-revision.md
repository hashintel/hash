# Divergence, correction, and contextual coexistence

## Short

Treat tensions as information and ask before reconciling them.

## Preservation rule

Do not average, silently choose, or use recency as universal truth when accounts diverge. Preserve the alternatives until their relationship is understood.

## Discriminating question

When a later account differs from an earlier one, establish whether it corrects the earlier account or whether both apply under different people, times, conditions, or purposes.

## Correction form

A genuine correction replaces the active account without erasing its history. A disagreement or contextual difference keeps both accounts visible until resolved.

## Ambiguity form

Ambiguity marks a place where meanings may diverge. Clarify it directly or through a discriminating concrete case; do not select the most convenient interpretation merely to continue.

## Notes

Preservation is strongly supported. The choice between direct clarification and case-based deepening remains unsettled and belongs in progressive teaching. The system prompt can safely state the prohibition against silent reconciliation.
