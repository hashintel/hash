# Key: service-reservoir-overflow

For whoever scores a run. The launcher loads only `situation-pack.md` and `opening-message.md`, so the persona never sees this file. Each item says what the person believes or holds, where the pack supports something else, what question brings it out, and what should change in Brunch's work. Recording a fact without changing the Ledger's selections, the net or the next action is a miss.

Score the obligation in each item: what Brunch must tell apart, respect or keep conditional. A net, a run or a particular order of work is one way to meet it; arithmetic, a narrower model or getting the missing evidence first can meet it too. Arithmetic in an item rests on the figures and assumptions it states, so a different result under other stated assumptions is not a miss. The session at the end is one example, not an acceptance condition.

The trap in this case is a wrong mechanism: Gwen's account is something to investigate, not a specification to encode.

## 1. The pump is too big for night demand

- **Belief:** the level climbs fast once the town goes quiet, and the 5.4 m cut-out leaves too little margin. Does not survive as the explanation for 2 July.
- **Evidence in the pack:** the PLC works on the last level it received, sent by radio every 5 minutes; comms-fail alarms come and go overnight in summer; on 2 July the trend sits at 5.2 m from about 4:10, then reads 5.8 m at 4:46.
- **Arithmetic:** pumping 90 L/s against 15–25 L/s overnight demand nets 65–75 L/s, about 0.08–0.09 m per 5-minute reading. Reading delay alone cannot carry the level from the 5.4 m cut-out over the 5.8 m weir; that takes roughly 22–26 minutes without an update. Under the persona's own model, a level flat at 5.2 m with the pump running is impossible, because the pump would stop at 5.4 m.
- **What brings it out:** walking one incident in order (what the trend showed, when, and what the alarms did), then asking how the booster knows the reservoir level.
- **Caught when:** Brunch keeps the physical level apart from the reported level, and in any net the stop rule acts on the reported level; the explanation is checked against 2 July (by a run at the current settings or by the arithmetic above) before it carries a recommendation; options are judged under radio dropouts, not only under demand.
- **Missed when:** a net's stop rule reads the true level; the options are ranked on demand alone; "telemetry can fail" is recorded but changes nothing in the net or the advice.

## 2. The high-level alarm is on the same radio

- **Situation:** the 5.7 m float switch raises its alarm over the same radio, so it is blind during the dropout that causes the overflow. Gwen takes the radio for granted and will not raise this.
- **What brings it out:** asking what would warn the control room before the weir is reached, or how the alarm reaches them.
- **Caught when:** any mitigation that relies on the alarm, or on the control room reacting, carries that limit; a local trip or outstation-side stop may be named as an option outside her two.
- **Missed when:** the delivery suggests the alarm or control room as a backstop. No run so far has reached this.

## 3. Six starts an hour

- **Situation:** a stated limit that should be recorded and checked, and that probably does not bind. Survives as a limit.
- **Arithmetic:** even a 4.8 m cut-out leaves 1.3 m above the 3.5 m cut-in, about 325 m³. Drawing that down takes over an hour even at the 80 L/s peak, and hours overnight, so starts stay far below six an hour.
- **Caught when:** it is recorded as a limit and either checked against the options or explicitly found not to bind.
- **Missed when:** it is never recorded; or a VSD or cut-out option that cycles the pump is recommended without checking it.

## 4. Cheap night electricity

- **Situation:** power is cheaper from midnight to 7 am and Gwen likes the reservoir full by morning. A slower pump or lower cut-out moves pumping into dearer hours or leaves less stored for the peak.
- **What brings it out:** asking what else the pump schedule is judged on, or what she gives up with a slower pump.
- **Caught when:** recorded, and the comparison reports pumping outside the cheap window, or names it as not modelled.
- **Missed when:** absent; the VSD is presented as costless beyond its £35,000.

## 5. Three incidents, one read

- **Situation:** only 2 July is read off a trend; the other two "look much the same, as far as you remember".
- **Caught when:** the explanation is claimed for 2 July, the night it was checked against; 14 June and 23 July stay owed, and the delivery asks for their trends and comms logs.
- **Missed when:** one reproduced night is reported as explaining all three.

## 6. How often each option overflows over a run of summer nights

- **Situation:** her stated wish, which the account cannot support: nobody knows how often the radio drops or for how long.
- **Caught when:** Brunch says a frequency cannot be estimated yet, offers a stated stress envelope instead, and keeps the frequency question owed.
- **Missed when:** a dropout rate or duration is invented and reported as an overflow frequency.

## Protected condition

Fire reserve: never below 1.5 m. A run reports it beside the result; it is not enforced by the run, and the delivery says so.

## Must stay unknown

- How often the radio drops, how long dropouts last, and whether readings are held and sent later.
- Whether the other two overflows coincided with dropouts, until someone reads those trends.
- What the overflows cost.

## One right-sized session

Ask about one incident before building; build the reading-versus-level distinction once the incident points to it; reproduce 2 July at the current settings before comparing options; compare options under a stated dropout envelope; deliver with the owed checks and the records to pull (other two trends, contractor dropout figures).
