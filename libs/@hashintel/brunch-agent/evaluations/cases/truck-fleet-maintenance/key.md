# Key: truck-fleet-maintenance

For whoever scores a run. The launcher loads only `situation-pack.md` and `opening-message.md`, so the persona never sees this file. Each item says what the person believes or holds, where the pack supports something else, what question brings it out, and what should change in Brunch's work. Recording a fact without changing the Ledger's selections, the net or the next action is a miss.

Score the obligation in each item: what Brunch must tell apart, respect or keep conditional. A net, a run or a particular order of work is one way to meet it; arithmetic, a narrower model or getting the missing evidence first can meet it too. Arithmetic in an item rests on the figures and assumptions it states, so a different result under other stated assumptions is not a miss. The session at the end is one example, not an acceptance condition.

The trap in this case is a plausible plan that must be revised once constraints are elicited, plus tacit rules: Nora's whiteboard and habits are something to investigate, not a specification to encode.

## 1. The whiteboard plan is basically fine

- **Belief:** highest score first, two trucks a day across two bays, gives a sound week that only needs comparing with alternatives. Does not survive as drawn.
- **Situation:** Monday CR-12 brakes and CR-19 engine; Tuesday CR-27 tyres and CR-72 brakes; Wednesday CR-34 brakes and an open bay. Harrowell Tuesday on CR-12, motorway Wednesday on CR-19, Monday's late-morning urban round on CR-72. Monday is workable (Milo on CR-12, Priya on CR-19, one brake kit and the engine pack). The rest is not:
  - Tuesday's two jobs are both Milo's (tyres and brakes), so they cannot run together; one bay waits for him.
  - Tuesday is the Harrowell mountain day, and the plan books the only recovery operator into both Tuesday bays. Nora's own goal is to keep recovery capacity free on mountain days; a call-out stops both Tuesday jobs.
  - Three brake jobs (CR-12, CR-72, CR-34) against one brake kit set aside. CR-12 uses it Monday; Tuesday and Wednesday depend on deliveries the whiteboard does not mention.
  - CR-12 on Harrowell depends on Monday's brake job finishing. If it slips (a recovery, or a breakdown taking the only brake kit, as CR-53 did last month), CR-12 stays at 82 and her own over-70 rule rules it out. The whiteboard names no fallback.
  - The whiteboard puts CR-72 on Monday's late-morning urban round because it is at the depot, but its returning driver cannot take the round, and other cover depends on dispatch (item 5).
- **Evidence in the pack:** the whiteboard section; parts set aside Monday (one brake kit, one matched steer-tyre set, one engine sensor/actuator pack); Milo does brakes, tyres and recovery, Priya engine and electrical; a planned service takes about 5 hours; deliveries usually next-day, specialised items several days; "Harrowell Tue" and "early Tuesday" mountain contract; CR-53 used the only brake kit in stock.
- **Arithmetic:** Tuesday is about 2 × 5 = 10 hours of Milo's work, done one after the other, not side by side. Brake kits needed this week: 3; set aside: 1; short by 2. Eligible Harrowell fallbacks under the over-70 rule and not otherwise committed Tuesday: CR-34 (58, back from the mountain Monday afternoon), CR-41 (37), CR-53 (31, roadside-repaired), CR-68 (22); each still needs a rested driver from dispatch.
- **What brings it out:** asking her to walk the whiteboard day by day: who works on each truck, which part it takes, where Milo is on Tuesday, what goes to Harrowell if Monday's job slips, who drives each truck.
- **Caught when:** Brunch revises the allocation, not just annotates it: Milo is not double-booked on Tuesday and recovery cover on the mountain day is dealt with; brake jobs beyond the first are tied to a kit delivery whose timing is owed; Harrowell has a fallback truck with brakes at 70 or under, conditional on dispatch confirming a rested driver. Checking the plan against the elicited constraints by hand shows the strain as well as a run does.
- **Missed when:** the whiteboard is encoded as the baseline and reported with caveats ("check parts", "Milo may be busy") while the allocation stays as drawn; or alternatives are compared on scores and bay count alone.

## 2. Two bays means two jobs together

- **Belief:** the workshop has two parallel slots. Does not survive.
- **Evidence in the pack:** two bays, but jobs need the right technician by skill; Milo is also the recovery operator, and brake and tyre work waits while he is out even with a bay empty; now and then a job needs both technicians; parts are limited and a breakdown can consume one set aside for a planned job.
- **What brings it out:** asking who does which jobs, what happens to the workshop when a truck breaks down on the road, and what last month's CR-53 day did to the bays.
- **Caught when:** whatever judges the week, a net or a check by hand, treats the workshop as bays plus technicians by skill plus parts, with recovery drawing on Milo; planned and roadside work share those resources.
- **Missed when:** the workshop is two identical servers, or technicians and parts are recorded but constrain neither the plan nor the net.

## 3. Scores are probabilities

- **Situation:** the dashboard's 0–100 numbers are "risk scores", not probabilities; nobody knows what chance of failure an 80 means, how score relates to time to failure, or whether brakes, engine and tyres compare cleanly. Nora reads them as rankings and a red line.
- **What brings it out:** asking what an 80 means, or how often a truck at a given score actually fails.
- **Caught when:** scores are treated as rankings of unknown calibration. Any result that depends on how score relates to failure states that relation as an assumption and says how the result moves if it changes. Revising the week on feasibility (parts, technicians, drivers, the Harrowell rule) needs no such relation, and leaving failure risk out, with that said, is not a miss. Calibration is an owed question for the vendor.
- **Missed when:** 82 becomes an 82% (or 8.2%) seven-day failure chance with no flag; the three components are summed or compared as if on one scale; or a recommendation rests on one assumed mapping presented as what the dashboard means.

## 4. Harrowell gets no truck with brakes over 70

- **Situation:** a tacit rule since CR-53, held in her head, not in the dispatch system. Survives as a rule; Nora will not raise it unless asked how she picks the Harrowell truck.
- **Evidence in the pack:** "What you take for granted"; "Harrowell does not slip"; CR-12 at 82 is the normal first choice.
- **What brings it out:** asking how she chooses the truck for the Harrowell run, or what would stop CR-12 going.
- **Caught when:** the rule constrains which truck goes to Harrowell, in the net if one is built, and the Harrowell assignment respects it, including after a slipped service.
- **Missed when:** Harrowell is assigned by score rank or the 80 red line only; the rule is recorded but no assignment or comparison uses it.

## 5. A spare truck is a spare run

- **Situation:** dispatchers say "spare truck" when the gap is often a rested driver. Nora checks both, but the whiteboard puts CR-72 on Monday's late-morning urban round because "it's at the depot", with the driver column blank.
- **Evidence in the pack:** a pairing is blocked at 9 hours driving and needs 11 hours rest; CR-72's driver hit the limit and got back about 05:30 Monday; the roster is not hers.
- **Arithmetic:** 05:30 + 11 hours = 16:30, so that pairing cannot leave late morning.
- **What brings it out:** asking who drives each truck on the whiteboard, or what "available" means for CR-72.
- **Caught when:** dispatch is judged on truck and rested driver together. CR-72's returning driver cannot take the late-morning round, so the round goes ahead only if dispatch confirms another rested driver, on CR-72 or another truck; the roster is an owed question for the transport manager.
- **Missed when:** truck availability alone decides dispatch; CR-72's returning driver is put on the round; or a driver is named or assumed free without the roster.

## 6. CR-53 at 74

- **Situation:** CR-53 seized a brake caliper on a mountain descent with a brake score of 74, under the red line. Tow about 4 hours, about 12 workshop hours, the only brake kit consumed, CR-27's tyre job deferred two days, the load lost.
- **What brings it out:** asking about the last roadside failure, or how a breakdown plays through the workshop.
- **Caught when:** the incident shapes how a breakdown plays through the week (recovery takes Milo, a bay is held for a long repair, a set-aside part is consumed, planned work is pushed), in the net if one is built or in the plan's fallbacks; it informs any failure-risk assumption without settling it.
- **Missed when:** Brunch concludes the dashboard is miscalibrated, or that 74 should have been red; or the incident is filed as a story with no effect on the plan or the net.

## 7. The mountain is the brake killer

- **Belief:** partly survives. Mountain descents wear brakes about 2.5 times as fast as the flat, but CR-19's engine score rose faster on loaded motorway runs than in its mountain week, and CR-27's biggest jump was tyres after urban roadworks.
- **Caught when:** where the work models wear, it depends on run type per component, not mountain-brakes only.
- **Missed when:** only mountain runs raise risk, or only brakes.

## Protected condition

Harrowell does not slip; driver hours (9 hours driving, then 11 hours rest) are never broken. A run reports both beside its result; any schedule that leaves Harrowell without an eligible truck and rested driver, or dispatches a pairing over its hours, is flagged, not traded off against cost.

## Must stay unknown

- How the vendor calibrates scores and what chance of failure a score means.
- Whether the dashboard should have predicted CR-53's seizure.
- Reliable times for failures, tows, repairs and parts deliveries, including when a brake kit ordered Monday would arrive.
- A single agreed cost for late deliveries, refused loads, early part replacement or idle workshop time.
- When board loads turn up, and the driver roster.

## One right-sized session

Ask her to walk the whiteboard before building; elicit technicians by skill, Milo's recovery duty, parts on hand, driver pairing and the Harrowell rule as the walk exposes them; check the whiteboard against them and revise it (Milo free on Tuesday, brake jobs tied to deliveries, a Harrowell fallback conditional on a rested driver); build a net only where comparing schedules under breakdowns needs one, stating any score-to-failure relation as an assumption; deliver with the owed checks (vendor calibration, brake kit lead time, driver roster).
