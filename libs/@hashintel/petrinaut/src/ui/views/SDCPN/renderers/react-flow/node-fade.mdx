The fade of a node draws a node outside the focused neighbourhood at 0.4 of its strength, with no opacity on the node or on a group inside it. [The canvas](doc:ui.views.canvas/canvas) says which nodes are in the neighbourhood, and [the arc layer](doc:ui.views.canvas.react-flow/arc-layer) fades the arcs.
This page is longer than a guide should be: it lists every place where a faded node is not the picture of a node under an opacity.

![A node says what it draws with a class per part, the pane holds one rule per class for a node without a role while a focus is active, and a firing asks whether its node is faded](./node-fade.d2)

## Entry point

`paneStyle` in `react-flow-canvas/pane-style.ts` is the class of the element around React Flow. `ReactFlowCanvas` sets `data-focus-active` on that element while the scene has a focus, and React Flow's wrapper of a node with a role carries `canvas-focus-role`. Every rule of the fade starts with the pane that has a focus and a node that has no role and is not selected. `insideFadedNode` in `styles/node-fade.ts` is that selector for code to ask with.

## Interface

A node says what it draws with these classes, which `styles/node-fade.ts` names:

| What a node draws                          | Class          | While the node is faded                                             |
| ------------------------------------------ | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The card or box                            | `node-surface` | Its fill, `--node-fill`, at 0.4, the ring of a classic box at 0.4   |
| Text                                       | `node-text`    | Its colour at 0.4: `--node-text`, or the colour it inherits         |
| A background of its own                    | `node-fill`    | `--node-fill` at 0.4                                                |
| A border                                   | `node-border`  | `--node-border` at 0.4                                              |
| A background nearly the colour of the card | `node-inlay`   | Not drawn                                                           |
| A token count, the pin of a port           | `node-cover`   | Opaque, mixed 0.4 toward the colour of the canvas                   |
| The shapes of an icon                      | none           | `fill-opacity` and `stroke-opacity` 0.4, no stroke on a filled icon |

`node-folds` marks a part that times its own changes, and the pane gives it no transition. `node-bolt` marks the bolt of a firing, which the rule for icons leaves alone. `data-arc-target` is on the handle of a node while the outline of an arc's target is drawn around it.

## Data flow

1. A hover rests on an item, or the selection changes. The scene _says whether a focus is active_ and _gives a node with a role its class_. React renders the nodes whose role changed and no other.
2. `ReactFlowCanvas` _sets the attribute_ on the pane. The browser restyles what a rule of the pane ends in: each part by its class, and each shape of an icon.
3. Each rule _draws_ its part _at 0.4 by its own colour_. Each part runs a transition of that colour over `--canvas-focus-duration`, on the main thread.
4. A transition that fires _asks whether its node is faded_ and _flashes as faded as the node_.

## Rules

**No opacity goes on a node, or on an element inside one that draws more than one thing.** The browser draws such an element into a buffer of its own on every frame of a pan, a zoom or a drag, and a net has one per node. "puts no opacity on a node or on anything inside one" in `pane-style.test.ts` reads the rules of the pane, and "draws nothing that the pane does not fade" in `faded-node-parts.test.tsx` fails for an opacity between 0 and 1 on a part.

**Every part of a node carries the class of what it draws.** A part without one stays at full strength in a faded node. `faded-node-parts.test.tsx` draws every component of `node-types.ts` with each node of its list. It fails for a property that is not known to draw nothing, on an element without the class that fades that property. It also fails for a pseudo-element that draws, for a style written in place that draws, for an element that is neither a `div`, a `span` nor a `button`, and for text outside `node-text`. The test reads the class names Panda gives a style. It does not draw a faded node: [Editor performance on a large net](doc:editor-performance) has the pictures.

**No rule changes what an element hands down to the elements inside it.** A custom property or a `color` on a faded node restyles every element of every node, at the change of focus and on every frame of the fade. So a colour a rule has to mix is a custom property its element sets once, `node-text` goes on the element that holds the text, and the fade of an icon is set on each shape. "sets no custom property and no inherited colour on an element that holds others" in `pane-style.test.ts` holds it.

**No rule ends in a class that every handle has.** At a change of focus the browser looks again at every element under the pane that a rule could apply to by its last selector, whatever comes before it. A rule for the handle under the pointer that ends in the class of a handle restyles the handles of every node. So the outline of an arc's target is found by `data-arc-target`, which one handle has, and "ends no rule in a class that every handle of every node has" holds it. The rule for icons ends in the shapes, which the paths of the arcs and of the minimap are too: a shape of an icon has no class of its own.

**A faded node has one strength.** `fadedNodeStrength` names it for the keyframes of a firing, and the rules of the pane spell it out, because Panda reads a style as it is written. "draws every part of a faded node at the one strength" holds the two equal.

Where two things of a node lie on each other, two alphas show the lower one through the upper. Four parts are drawn another way for that reason:

- The icon box of a transition or of a component instance is an inlay. It is nearly the white of its card, so the card under it draws that colour and the box is left out. A firing flash changes the colour of the card, so the animation draws the box for as long, as a cover.
- The icon box of a compact place lies on the colour of a token type. The fill of a faded place is a gradient with a hole under the box. A gradient does not fade, so the colour under it does, and the gradient goes once the fade back is over.
- A token count and a pin are covers. Each lies across the border of its node, and a count is white on black. They stay opaque and take the colour they would have over the bare canvas.
- A filled icon is a fill under a stroke of one colour, and the inner half of the stroke would show as a darker rim. Its stroke goes.

## Pitfalls

A faded node is not the picture of a node under an opacity in these places.

At rest, while it is faded:

- A part that lies on the fill of its node lets 36% of what is behind the node through, where the whole node let 60% through: its text, the glyph of a classic transition and the disc of a badge. It shows where an arc passes behind a faded node.
- Such a part is also darker and takes the tint of the fill, by 0.24 of the difference between the canvas and the fill in each channel. On the green of a place of the Supply Chain example a label is 35 of 255 darker in one channel.
- A token count and a pin hide what is behind them.
- A filled icon is smaller by half the width of its stroke, and the corners of the transition's square are sharp.
- A faded node has no elevation, and none more under the pointer.
- One device pixel along the inner edge of a card's border, and of the ring of a classic box, is darker: the fill stops where the border begins, and the two meet in that pixel.
- The dot a node under the pointer offers an arc from is at full strength, and so is the outline of keyboard focus on a handle an arc can land on.

While the fade runs:

- The hairline of an icon box, the rule under a component's header, the white ring of a pin and an inlay change at the start of a fade. Everything else takes the time of the fade.
- A token count and a pin are darker midway than under an opacity: the browser mixes their two colours in Oklab.
- When the focus moves straight from one item to another, the ring of a node that joins the neighbourhood arrives a little sooner, and the ring of one that leaves it goes a little later.
- A browser without `transition-behavior: allow-discrete` drops the gradient of a compact place at the start of the fade back, and the fill comes back from nothing.
- The fade is a transition of each colour, on the main thread, where the opacity of a node ran on the compositor. A small net shows as many frames of it. A large net shows a few steps.

Under the pointer and in a run:

- The border of a compact transition or of a component card takes the time of the fade to darken under the pointer, as the ring of a classic box does: one transition serves the hover and the fade.
- A flash is as faded as its node was when the firing landed. A focus that changes during the flash does not change it.
- The glyph of a faded classic transition takes a tint of the flash behind it.
