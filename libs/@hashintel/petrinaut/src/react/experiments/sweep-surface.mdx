The surface view shows the final value of one metric over two swept
parameters as a filled contour. It draws from the cache that the
[navigator](doc:react.experiments/parameter-sweeps) refines: a point on the
surface is a point the sweep computed. A sweep with fewer than two swept
parameters has no surface.

![Every point the sweep finishes becomes a visited cell, and the surface draws the visited cells and nothing it sampled itself.](./sweep-surface-feed.d2)

## The sweep feeds the surface

When a batch of a point selection finishes, the
sweep session adds it to its cache and publishes the point as a visited
cell: its quantized position and the value of each metric over the finished
runs. The type is `SweepVisitedCell`, and the list is `sweep.visited` on the
record. The list is a new array only when a batch finishes.

Points arrive three ways: a slider of the navigator, a click or a drag on the
plot, and an optimizer navigating trial by trial through `navigateSweep`. A
range selection goes into the cache too, but it is no point, so it draws
nothing.

`visitedSurfaceField` projects every visited point onto the two shown axes at
its fractional grid coordinate (`surfaceGridCoordinate`). A point is a filled
dot with the value of the shown metric. The point that matches the selection
is emphasized, and a point where the metric never reported is a muted ring.

`computingSurfaceField` adds the point being computed as a ring. Its value
joins the field from the visited list once the batch finishes, so the contour
does not follow the metric's course over time while a batch streams.

Points computed at other values of the hidden axes are drawn too, projected,
as a study's trials are. The picture is everything computed so far and no
slice of it. Where two visited points share the shown coordinates, the later
one gives the value. Switching the shown metric reads the same visited cells
again. The caption (`describeVisitedSurface`) counts the points sampled. It
adds "sampling" while a batch computes, and "choosing next point" while an
optimizer drives and no batch computes.

The plot is also a control. A dark ring marks the navigator's position. A
press and drag shows a crosshair, positioned in percentages so that a pointer
move never repaints the field, and the caption reads the two parameter
values at the pointer.
Releasing selects a point, as a click does: the two shown axes at the pick,
and each hidden axis at the middle of its selected range. While an optimizer
drives the sweep the card takes the `optimizing` tone and the plot takes no
pick.

## Drawing a field from few points

Most cells are unsampled at any moment, so the picture has to read at every
stage. Inverse distance weighting over the sampled points gives smooth areas
around the first samples, which approach the surface as cells arrive.
Marching squares over that raster draws the iso-lines. Sampled combinations
are marked with dots, so it stays visible where data exists.

The interpolation and the iso-lines are pure functions with tests of their
own, beside the `ContourSurface` component. The component paints their result
onto a canvas.

A change of axis or metric starts another field, and the plot drops the
previous picture, which shows another quantity. Where the values of one
field are cleared and fewer than three samples exist, the plot keeps the
previous picture, dimmed to 45%, with the dots on top. One or two samples
interpolate to a nearly uniform fill that says less than the previous
picture.

## A point's precision is its ladder depth

A visited point's value comes from the last frame that has samples: the mean
of the distribution for a distribution metric, and the frame's value for a
scalar one (`sweepCellSample`). A net that terminates finishes its runs
before `maxTime`, so its final frames carry no samples, and the literal last
frame would give no value for any point of such a net. Runs that ended
earlier are absent from the frame that is read, so where runs end at
different times the value weights the runs that lasted longest.

A point the navigator stays on climbs the ladder to the run budget. A point
an optimizer visited stops at the trial's run cap, which is the study's runs
per step, until the study settles on it or the navigator returns to it. The
deeper result goes into the same cache and the surface repaints with it.

## The optimizer on the surface

A study draws on this surface and no other. Its steps are points the sweep
visited through `navigateSweep`, so each arrives as a dot at the trial's run
cap, and the step being computed is the ring. An infeasible draw never
reaches the surface, because the evaluator prunes it before it navigates.

While the study drives the sweep, the card only displays. Once the study
settles, the navigator rests on the best point. **Stop** on the Parameters
card parks the navigator at once on the last point tried, and the best point
follows only when the study's last event carries one. In both cases the plot
takes picks again, and the point refines to the run budget through the same
cache.
[Sweep orchestration](doc:react.experiments/sweep-orchestration) describes
the study that drives that navigation and the constraints it enforces.
