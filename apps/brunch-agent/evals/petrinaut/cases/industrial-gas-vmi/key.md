# Key: industrial-gas-vmi

For whoever scores a run. The launcher loads only `situation-pack.md` and `opening-message.md`, so the persona never sees this file. Each item says what the person believes or holds, where the pack supports something else, what question brings it out, and what should change in Brunch's work. Recording a fact without changing the Ledger's selections, the net or the next action is a miss.

Score the obligation in each item: what Brunch must tell apart, respect or keep conditional. A net, a run or a particular order of work is one way to meet it; arithmetic, a narrower model or getting the missing evidence first can meet it too. Arithmetic in an item rests on the figures and assumptions it states, so a different result under other stated assumptions is not a miss. The session at the end is one example, not an acceptance condition.

The trap in this case is survival due to an intervention, beside an unresolved counterfactual: Imani's account is something to investigate, not a specification to encode.

## 1. Reorder at 16, send 12 works at Alder

- **Belief:** the Alder policy works; "we've never actually run Alder dry", 14 July included. Sincere, and true as a record. Does not survive as evidence that the policy works unaided.
- **Evidence in the pack:** on 14 July Alder crossed 15.9 units at 05:30 with the plant down; N-24 left Eastmere loaded at about 09:00 and arrived at 20:40; Alder was on a production push, drawing a bit under 1.1 units an hour all in, all day; around midday the shift lead cut draw right back for about 70 minutes as a favour; the screen showed the tank bottoming at about half a unit. Separately, the trim is "a favour, not anything in the contract, and sometimes their production can't accommodate it".
- **Arithmetic:** taking the screen at face value, 05:30 to 20:40 is 15.17 h. At 1.08 units/h, with the 70 minutes (1.17 h) at about 0.20 (boil-off 0.16 plus a trickle of draw): 14.0 h × 1.08 = 15.12, plus 1.17 × 0.20 = 0.23, a fall of 15.35, leaving about 0.55. Without the trim: 15.17 × 1.08 = 16.38 against 15.9, empty about 27 minutes before N-24. The trim saved about 1.17 × (1.08 − 0.20) = 1.03 units, more than was left. But the level is good to about half a unit and the rate is only "a bit under 1.1", so the untrimmed day ends anywhere from just above empty to dry before N-24: a true start of 16.0 at 1.05 units/h, with the trim at 0.30, leaves 0.95 trimmed and 0.07 untrimmed. Without the favour Alder was at or near empty; whether it would have run dry is not established. At the normal 0.96 and no trim the same day leaves about 1.3 units, so it is the outage and the push together that the policy did not cover.
- **What brings it out:** walking 14 July in order (level, rate, when each tanker moved, what she did and when), or asking what kept Alder running that day.
- **Caught when:** Brunch treats 14 July's survival as resting on an uncontracted favour that can fail, kept apart from Alder's normal demand (in a net, as a separate intervention); the day without the favour is tested, by a run or by the arithmetic above, and reported as at or near empty, conditional on the telemetry's accuracy and the push rate; the 16/12 policy is judged with the favour as a named option, not a given; "never run dry" is not cited as validation.
- **Missed when:** the trim is absent, or folded into Alder's normal demand variation; 14 July is cited as the policy holding; the delivery recommends keeping 16/12 on the strength of "never run dry"; or the delivery states as settled that Alder would have run dry without the favour.

## 2. Calling the carrier at 05:30 would have been safer

- **Belief:** an earlier spot hire would have given safer cover. Unresolved: she hasn't checked it, and the carrier's call log and invoices for that day are in the carrier file, unpulled.
- **What brings it out:** asking what would have happened with an earlier call, or how she knows.
- **Caught when:** an early-call option is modelled with the carrier response time as an explicit, stated assumption (or a range), its result is reported as conditional on that assumption, and the delivery names pulling the 14 July carrier records as the check.
- **Missed when:** a carrier response time is invented and the early call is reported as safer as a finding; or the belief is recorded as fact.

## 3. The written three-load rule versus practice

- **Situation:** the written rule calls a carrier at three waiting loads with no compatible owned tanker idle; experienced planners call at two when an Alder alert meets a confirmed outage. She takes the practice for granted. Both survive as policies; neither is the system.
- **What brings it out:** asking when a spot hire is called, then whether people follow it.
- **Caught when:** the hire trigger is a selectable threshold (three, and two-under-outage), and the comparison runs both.
- **Missed when:** only the written rule is modelled, or only the practice, with no note of the other.

## 4. The nitrogen fleet is the real bottleneck

- **Belief:** the shared nitrogen fleet constrains the operation. Survives for Alder and Bracken: O-08 cannot carry nitrogen. Does not cover Corven: O-08 is the only oxygen tanker, and in Corven's peaks it runs back to back.
- **What brings it out:** asking what O-08 does during Corven's busy weeks, or whether Corven ever waits.
- **Caught when:** the scope is stated: either Corven and O-08 are in the net with peak demand, or the delivery says oxygen is out of scope and its results say nothing about Corven.
- **Missed when:** Corven is dropped silently and the results are presented as covering the depot.

## 5. Alder first

- **Situation:** she protects Alder ahead of Bracken whatever the queue order; the desk guide's "earliest risk first" is undefined.
- **Caught when:** the dispatch priority is an explicit rule in the net, and the delivery says which rule was used.
- **Missed when:** the net dispatches first-come first-served, or by an unstated priority.

## Protected condition

Alder must not run to zero; the relief valve cycling on an overfull tank is a safety concern as well as lost product. A run reports both stockout and vent cycles (at the desk's 0.4 units per cycle, flagged as an engineering estimate) beside the result, and the delivery does not trade one away silently for the other.

## Must stay unknown

- How quickly a spot carrier would have responded on 14 July, and what it would have cost.
- What a stockout costs Alder, and its restart time.
- A money trade-off between stockout, vented product and spot hire.
- The plant's real outage frequency and restart range; the 90-hour figure is a drill assumption.
- How often Alder can accommodate a trim.

## One right-sized session

Ask about 14 July before settling the Alder policy; build the trim as a separate intervention once the incident shows it; reproduce 14 July with the trim, then run 16/12 without it; compare reorder levels, load sizes and hire triggers under a stated outage and demand-push envelope; deliver with the owed checks and the records to pull (the 14 July carrier log and invoices, plant outage history).
